## Appendix B — Provenance Index

*Draft v1. Per series convention. This index gathers the book's provenance system in one place: the tag definitions, a chapter-by-chapter register of what was claimed under each tag, the register of invented scenes, and — because Chapter 10 promised it — the full verification ledger of the swampland literature map on which that chapter's physics stands. In production, each register entry expands into full citations; this draft fixes the architecture and the commitments.*

### The tags

- **[VERIFIED]** — established physics or mathematics, or an independently confirmed empirical result, that holds regardless of string/M-theory's fate. (Examples: black-hole entropy scaling with area as theoretical result within semiclassical gravity; the 2015 gravitational-wave detection; the proven mirror theorems; lunar reflectance and tidal locking.)
- **[SOURCED]** — consensus results, standard history, or established research literatures, cited in good faith to their standard sources. This is the workhorse tag, covering both the physics history (the duality revolution, the naming of M-theory) and the human sciences (attachment research, the anthropology of mourning, the choice-overload literature).
- **[BOUNDARY]** — the edge of anyone's knowledge: open problems, live controversies, and the places where this book's own analogies reach their tested limit. The final [BOUNDARY] in the book is set on its own thesis.
- **[ILLUSTRATIVE]** — an image doing image-work: organizing a question without asserting an isomorphism. The moon-mappings carry this tag throughout.
- ***Structural analogy*** — the book's central device, flagged per chapter: a claim that two systems share a shape at pattern level, offered because the shape is exactly statable in one domain and instructive in the other; never a claim that the physics evidences the human conclusion.
- **[FOUNDATIONAL]** — used in the Chapter 10 register, following the source map's convention: pre-arXiv or field-defining works cited by journal reference.

### The standing rules

1. Every chapter drafts its human argument first; the physics is fitted to it, never the reverse. The human argument must survive removal of the physics (the "removal test" of Appendix A).
2. No one-to-one mappings between the five models of the self and the five named string theories. The analogy lives at pattern level only. This refusal is stated in Chapters 1, 2, and here.
3. Each of the five partial models returns once in Parts II–IV as a limit of the relational picture, never as a casualty (the Chapter 2 courtesy). Register: biological → Ch. 3; computational → Ch. 4; narrative → Ch. 5; social → Ch. 7; contemplative → Ch. 8.
4. The M-motifs (membrane, matrix, mirror/mystery, mother) are offered one per part and released; none is chosen (Ch. 11).
5. Invented scenes are disclosed in their chapters' provenance notes and registered below.

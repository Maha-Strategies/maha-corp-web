## Findings

The live trust report graded the endpoint B at 67.4, with a 53.3-73.0 score range and 0.436 confidence. A separately generated alternatives response represented the subject as 61.6/C. These are external point-in-time scoring outputs, not a Maha service-level objective, and their divergence means they should be treated as directional rather than as a single canonical score.

Strong signals:

- 31 of 31 scored probes succeeded.
- Uptime, x402 envelope compliance, and price stability were each reported as 100%.
- No flags were reported.
- The live challenge advertised x402 v2, Base Mainnet, native USDC, a 1,000-base-unit price, and the current settlement address.
- Five settlements totaling 0.005 USDC were observed, all from one distinct payer.

Score constraints that code cannot immediately repair:

- The endpoint had less than one observed day of history, depressing its age component.
- Settlement activity came from one payer, depressing economic reputation.
- Confidence was constrained by the short observation window.

Latency evidence:

- The trust response reported 310.87 ms average latency across 31 probes from one EU vantage point.
- The purchased history contained 30 latency samples: 168 ms minimum, 258.5 ms median, 312.5 ms mean, approximately 535.25 ms p95, and 785 ms maximum.
- These timings include network distance and cannot isolate Maha server execution time.

## Competitive interpretation

The alternatives service returned five semantically similar endpoints with point scores between 70.9 and 77.2. It did not test compression quality, evidence retention, citation traceability, or downstream cost avoided. The alternatives charged 0.002 or 0.008 USD per call; Maha advertised 0.001 USD. The result therefore indicates older or more economically active listings, not proof that their compression products outperform Maha.

## Remediation derived from the audit

1. Permit cross-origin browser clients to submit `PAYMENT-SIGNATURE`.
2. Expose `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` and `PAYMENT-RESPONSE` to browser JavaScript.
3. Cache the immutable, validated Bazaar declaration during a warm deployment instance so repeated unpaid probes do not rebuild the full schema.
4. Preserve the 0.001 USD price while collecting external-payer and repeat-use evidence.
5. Re-run the same audit only after a meaningful observation window or contract change; repeated self-payments do not create credible market evidence.

## Interpretation boundary

This audit establishes public availability, protocol compliance, price stability, and the monitoring service's observed settlement history. It does not establish answer quality, comparative compression performance, customer demand, or an independent security audit.

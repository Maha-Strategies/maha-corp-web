# Strong-domain scaling tranche

This tranche scales the parts of Maha that already have inspectable primary or
institutional sources. It does not lower evidence, review, privacy, or release
gates to increase a route count.

## Ranked path

1. **Tamil religion and source-text passage atlases.** Exact Project Madurai
   pāsuram and poem locators, plus open university scholarship, make this the
   highest-yield non-overlapping track. It also connects to the existing
   `textual-authority` discovery signal.
2. **Formal mathematics.** Claude's PR 400 added two source-first DLMF pages and
   moved the legacy inventory from 167 to 169. DLMF's permanent equation
   identifiers make this the best computation-linked lane, but it remains
   Claude's track to avoid duplication.
3. **Astrology traditions and celestial interpretation.** Generative-search
   clicks are an observed demand signal, and the site already contains 4
   traditions, 5 celestial guides, 42 calculation references, 36 timing
   references, 30 mundane/corporate references, and 12 tropical/sidereal
   comparisons. Expansion starts by mapping observed questions onto those 129
   modeled items and adds a page only where the answer is not already covered.
   Celestial facts and reproducible calculations, tradition-relative rules,
   historical claims, and empirical validation remain separate; no page may
   imply personalized prediction or scientific validation.
4. **Astronomy.** NASA, JPL, IAU, LIGO, and observatory sources are accessible
   and authoritative within bounded roles. Page yield is lower because an
   overview cannot stand in for survey-specific uncertainty.
5. **Evidence methodology.** High product relevance, but new pages must be
   worked examples rather than restatements of existing governance policies.

Semiconductor expansion is deferred. Its remaining evidence need is dominated
by institutional access, first-party disclosures, and proceedings rather than
open-source research throughput.

## Tiruvāymoḻi contract

The atlas covers twenty consecutive complete units from pāsurams 2791–3012.
Each page carries an exact range, poetic voice, name occurrences, passage
observations, five bounded questions, explicit limitations, and internal links.
The exceptional 2967–2979 unit remains at the length printed by the translation
rather than being forced into an assumed ten-verse pattern.

Every claim is phrased as a claim about Kausalya Hart's named translation or as
attributed scholarship. Nothing in the atlas treats translated wording as the
untranslated Tamil, devotional theology as an empirical result, or one unit as
the whole Tiruvāymoḻi.
